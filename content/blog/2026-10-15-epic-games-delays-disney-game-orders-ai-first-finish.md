Epic Games has delayed a shooter made in collaboration with Walt Disney and told the team finishing it to lean more heavily on artificial intelligence, according to people familiar with the project who spoke anonymously to [Bloomberg, which reported the postponed release and an internal AI-first designation](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-12/epic-games-delays-disney-project-asks-developers-to-use-more-ai). The game had been scheduled to arrive in March 2026, and no replacement date has been announced. Jason Schreier's account describes a production now labelled AI first. Developers have been asked to use more AI tools to complete the work.

The delay is the less interesting half. Slipped dates are routine in large game productions. What matters more is how a marquee, Disney-branded project will be finished. The change was made after the game was already well into production, and it was communicated to staff rather than to players.

That distinction matters because AI first is a statement about labour before it says anything about technology. The directive concerns the tools used to finish an existing game. This is not a fresh concept generated by a machine. In practice, the question is who absorbs the change: whether artists, writers and testers are trained to use the new tools, expected to use them, or find parts of their role disappearing. None of that is legible from the outside. The account exists only because unnamed sources spoke when nobody involved was authorised to do so. A shift in production methods at a studio whose every move is watched, attached to a globally recognised entertainment brand, is currently known through reporting rather than disclosure. That is a thin basis on which to judge what players will eventually receive.

Games are not classrooms. Still, the underlying problem is one we work on daily. Honest AI use in education rests on disclosure and verifiability: what was produced with assistance, by whom, and what evidence would let somebody check. An institution that adopts AI-first methods without saying which parts of the work changed invites the same suspicion now surrounding this production. The label travels; the account does not.

For any of this to matter beyond industry gossip, several things would need to become visible. What AI first means in specific tasks. Which parts of the finished game retain meaningful human authorship. What happened to the staff whose jobs the mandate reshapes. Whether the extra time the delay buys produces a demonstrably better game rather than a cheaper one is testable in principle. It is not testable by decree, and it will not be settled on the day the game eventually ships.
