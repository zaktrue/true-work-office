The instrument at the centre of the case is a memorandum, not a statute. National Security Presidential Memorandum 7, issued in September 2025 after the assassination of Charlie Kirk, directs federal law enforcement to investigate organisations and individuals that criticise administration policy. Defence attorneys for 15 Minnesota residents facing federal conspiracy charges have obtained records through discovery that show how that direction was executed. The material includes reports, audio transcripts, emails, intercepted Signal chats and photographs gathered under two named efforts, Project Whipple Shield and Operation Puppet Master, as [Wired's account of the disclosed surveillance records](https://www.wired.com/story/ice-is-running-one-of-the-largest-anti-left-surveillance-operations-in-us-history/) sets out.

Enforcement ran through an existing structure rather than a new one. Operation Keyhole, a Homeland Security Investigations undercover funding programme operating since 2008 across several midwestern states, supported six active investigations in the Minneapolis-Saint Paul area as of June 2026. About 120 HSI agents, 11 of them working undercover, were assigned. Operatives entered activist groups and their Signal chats, and agents deployed Reaper drones alongside tools normally reserved for counterterrorism and money-laundering inquiries. The prosecution's theory is that volunteer networks which tracked federal agents to warn communities during Operation Metro Surge amounted to a criminal conspiracy. Defence lawyers argue the charges reach well beyond the conduct alleged, and they draw a direct comparison with the FBI's COINTELPRO programme. Three former law enforcement officials said the case appears to be part of a wider intelligence-gathering effort predating the December 2025 deployment to Minnesota.

For universities and research groups, the consequential question is evidentiary rather than partisan. Work that studies protest, extremism or platform moderation depends on records whose provenance can be examined: who authorised collection, what was retained, and how a subject can challenge it. The same standard governs AI systems used in teaching and research, where logs and audit trails are often the only means of demonstrating that data handling was lawful and that findings can be reproduced. A programme built on intercepted private messages and aerial observation makes that standard considerably harder to meet, and it constrains what researchers can credibly promise the people they study.

The next measurable step is procedural. Defence motions may test whether the memorandum's authorisation survives judicial scrutiny, and whether the chain of custody behind the disclosed records holds up under challenge. The claim that a broader effort predates the Minnesota deployment remains asserted by former officials rather than evidenced in the filings described so far.
